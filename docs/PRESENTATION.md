# Presentation outline (about 8 minutes, 6 slides)

## 1. Recommendation (lead slide)
- **Use PCI 2.0 to benchmark and to pick what to investigate. Do not yet use it to rank reform spending.**
- At the default 3-year horizon no province gets a priority label. At 2 years, 16 of 34 do, almost all because of one group (transparency and compliance, p \u2248 0.049).
- Next step: test that one lead with firm-level or reform-timing data before any budget decision.

## 2. What the page does
- Compares a province with similar-income peers on five groups of PCI 2.0 indicators (gap in SD).
- Shows two past associations with new firms per 1000 residents: 2025 cross-section and 2018\u20132024 panel.
- Example, HCMC: largest gap is integrity and fair competition, 1.0 SD below 8 peers (recomputed independently); no reliable link to new firms.

## 3. How far to trust it
- Associations only: 34 provinces, perception data, fixed effects do not remove province-by-year shocks.
- Three recomputed numbers all reproduce (gap \u22121.0087; 2025 G1 coef 0.1675, SE 0.0937; panel G3 0.0359, SE 0.0175).
- The sub-indices do not predict new firms out of sample (LOO R\u00B2 \u2248 \u22120.02).

## 4. Five hard questions
Causality; old vs new PCI framework; multiple comparisons; assumed costs (change the top group for 7 of 32 provinces); policy relevance. Each is answered on the page as a visible limitation (L1\u2013L12) or flagged as unresolved.

## 5. What we changed after review
Headline no longer says "should invest"; Google Fonts removed; scenario warns that panel slopes come from the legacy PCI; limitations list added to the page.

## 6. Decisions requested
(a) Fix the horizon before looking at results. (b) Replace assumed costs with officials' estimates. (c) Collect reform dates to test timing.
